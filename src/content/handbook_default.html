2 Absence

We care about your health and wellbeing. This section explains what to do if you are unwell and unable to work.

Disabilities

You are not legally required to disclose a disability to us. However, if you do have a condition that may affect your work, we encourage you to let us know in confidence. This allows us to consider any reasonable adjustments that may help you. You do not need to share a diagnosis. Any information that helps us understand your needs is enough. We will never penalise you for choosing not to disclose.

How to report an absence

We understand that being unwell can make it difficult to follow a process. Where you can, please call your line manager personally by phone before your normal start time on your first day of absence. Please do not use text messages or social media unless there is genuinely no alternative. If you are too unwell to call, please ask someone to contact us on your behalf. If no contact is possible straight away, please reach out as soon as you reasonably can.

When you do make contact, please tell us why you are absent, when you expect to return, and flag any urgent work that needs attention. If you are still absent after day one, keep your line manager updated by 9am each day.

Certificates and fit notes

For absences of seven calendar days or fewer (counting weekends and non-working days), complete the SDI Self-Certification Form (available on SharePoint) when you return and give it to your line manager. If your absence continues beyond seven calendar days, you must provide a fit note covering your absence from the eighth day onwards. A fit note (a Statement of Fitness for Work) can be issued by a GP, hospital doctor, registered nurse, pharmacist, physiotherapist or occupational therapist. Send it to your line manager as soon as you receive it, and provide a further fit note to cover any continued absence so that no period is left uncertified. If you do not provide the required medical evidence, any sick pay (including SSP) may be withheld for the uncertified period, and the absence may be treated as unauthorised and managed under our absence and disciplinary procedures.

A fit note may say you are fit for work with adjustments such as a phased return, altered hours, or amended duties. We will consider any such recommendation and discuss options with you. If we cannot make the suggested adjustment, we will explain why and agree a review date. In that case we will treat the fit note as if it said you are not fit for work.

Independent medical examination

We may ask you, at our expense, to be assessed by an independent doctor. Refusing without good reason may lead to disciplinary action. Where necessary, we may also request a medical report from your own GP or consultant under the Access to Medical Reports Act 1988. We will explain your rights and obtain your written consent before doing so.

Holiday during sickness

If you want to take holiday while you are off sick, you must book it in the usual way and obtain your GP's agreement. Sick pay will not be paid during any period recorded as holiday. Any unused holiday entitlement that could not be taken because of long term sickness may be carried over, subject to statutory rules.

Sick pay

We know that being unwell is stressful enough without worrying about money. Here is a clear explanation of what you will be paid if you are off sick.

To qualify for Statutory Sick Pay (SSP), you must have notified your line manager before your normal start time on the first day of absence.

SSP is currently paid from day one of absence and for a maximum of 28 weeks.

During your probationary period

During probation, the company does not pay company sick pay. You may be entitled to Statutory Sick Pay (SSP), which is the government minimum. SSP currently starts from your first day of absence. Please speak to your line manager if you are unsure what this means for you.

After your probationary period

Once you have passed probation, if you are off sick the company will support you as follows, for up to 13 weeks in any rolling 12-month period:

  • Weeks 1 to 6: you will receive your full normal salary.
  • Weeks 7 to 13: you will receive half your normal salary.

In both cases, any SSP you are entitled to is included within these payments. You will not receive less than the figures above.

Beyond 13 weeks

If you are absent for more than 13 weeks, company sick pay stops and you will receive SSP only, for as long as you remain eligible under government rules. Any payment beyond this is entirely at the company's discretion.

What is a rolling 12 month period?

This means the 13-week entitlement is not fixed to a calendar year. For example, if you used 8 weeks of sick pay in October, you would have 5 weeks remaining until the following October, when those earlier weeks begin to fall away and your entitlement gradually rebuilds.

If you are ever unsure about your sick pay position, please speak to your line manager or a member of the leadership team. We would always rather you asked than worried.

Long term or persistent absence

We will be as supportive as we can if you are seriously or long term ill. However, if absence continues for an extended period or follows a pattern of concern, we may need to consider changes to your role or, as a last resort, the ending of your employment. We will always follow a fair process and give you the opportunity to discuss the situation.

Arriving late or leaving early

If travel disruption prevents you arriving on time or staying for your full shift, contact your line manager as soon as possible. Depending on the circumstances, your manager may agree one of the following:

  • Make up the time on another day.
  • Use annual leave.
  • Take unpaid leave (pay adjusted accordingly).
  • Be paid as normal.
  • Work from home.

Jury service and public duties

Notify us as soon as you receive a jury summons. Leave will normally be granted. There is no contractual entitlement to full pay during jury service, however any allowances paid to you by the court must be declared so they can be offset against any discretionary pay we award.

Please ensure this absence is recorded in Timetastic by you or your line manager immediately.

Reserve Armed Forces

You may use your paid annual leave for reserve duties. Any additional time off is unpaid and at the company's discretion.

We value the commitment and skills that reservists bring to their work and to the company.

Please ensure this absence is recorded in Timetastic by you or your line manager immediately.

Medical appointments

Wherever possible, please schedule appointments outside working hours or at the start or end of the day. All appointments must be pre-approved by your line manager and should not exceed two hours. Pay during attendance at such appointments is at the company's discretion.

Please ensure this absence is recorded in Timetastic by you or your line manager immediately.

Dependant emergencies

You have the right to reasonable unpaid time off to deal with an unexpected emergency involving someone who depends on you (for example, a child, partner, or elderly relative). Examples include:

  • Sudden illness, injury, or assault involving a dependant.
  • Breakdown of care arrangements.
  • Death of a dependant.
  • An unexpected incident involving a child at school.

Call your line manager personally before the start of your shift, explain the situation, and give an expected return date.

Behaviour while absent

We ask that while you are absent, you focus on your recovery. Please avoid activities that could delay your return to health, such as working for another employer or undertaking physical activity that is inconsistent with your reason for absence. We want you back when you are genuinely well.

Return to work meetings

After any absence, your line manager will usually hold a brief return to work meeting to check you are fit to return, understand the reason for absence, and agree any support needed. If you are taking medication that could affect your ability to work safely, please mention it at this meeting.

Falsifying absence claims

We trust our employees and take our duty of care seriously. If, however, we have reasonable grounds to believe that absence has been taken dishonestly, we will investigate fully and fairly. Where dishonesty is confirmed, disciplinary action will follow, which may include dismissal for gross misconduct.

Special unpaid leave

In exceptional circumstances, we may agree to grant unpaid leave for personal reasons such as family responsibilities or education. You must use your paid annual leave first. Requests must be submitted in writing directly to the Chair & CEO and must include your reasons and the dates you are requesting. Special unpaid leave is entirely at our discretion and may be declined or withdrawn.

Please ensure your absence is recorded in Timetastic (our online staff planner).

3 Alcohol, Drugs and Smoking

The company is committed to the health, safety, and wellbeing of all staff. As a remote-first organisation without a fixed office, this policy applies during all working hours, at staff meetings, away days, client visits, and any other work-related event or gathering.

Alcohol

Alcohol is not permitted during normal working hours. It is, however, permitted at company social events, away days, staff meetings, and other occasions where it has been expressly authorised by the Chair & CEO.

Regardless of the occasion, employees are expected to drink responsibly. Attending work, a client visit, or any work-related engagement under the influence of alcohol is classed as gross misconduct and may result in dismissal. Excessive or irresponsible drinking at company events, or behaviour that brings the company into disrepute, will also be treated as a disciplinary matter.

Drugs

Using, possessing, or distributing illegal drugs during working hours, at staff meetings, away days, client visits, or any work-related event is strictly forbidden and will be treated as gross misconduct. If you are prescribed medication that may affect your ability to work safely, please speak to the Chair & CEO in confidence.

We reserve the right to search employees and their belongings if there are reasonable grounds to suspect a breach of this policy. Refusing to cooperate with a search may itself be treated as gross misconduct.

Smoking and Vaping

SDI does not have a fixed company premises. However, all venues used for staff meetings, away days, client visits, and any other work-related gathering are treated as smoke free and vapour free environments. This applies to all employees, customers, visitors, and contractors. Company vehicles, where used, are also smoke free and vapour free.

4 Bereavement Pay and Leave

If you lose a child under the age of 18, and you are the child's legal parent or had day to day caring responsibility for the child, you are entitled to two weeks of statutory parental bereavement leave. The entitlement applies if either condition is met — you do not need to satisfy both. The same applies if you have suffered a stillbirth after 24 weeks of pregnancy.

Key points:

  • Leave must be taken as one block of two weeks, or as two separate one week blocks.
  • Leave must be taken within 56 weeks of the child's death.
  • Notice for leave in the first seven weeks: give notice before the leave begins.
  • Notice from the eighth week onwards: one week's notice is required.
  • Leave can be cancelled or rescheduled using the same notice periods.
  • Statutory Parental Bereavement Pay applies if you have been continuously employed for 26 weeks up to the week before the child's death and meet National Insurance thresholds.

For bereavement involving other close family members, please speak to your line manager. Short periods of paid or unpaid compassionate leave are at the Chair & CEO's discretion.

12 Data Protection

We take our data protection obligations seriously and comply with the UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR) and the Data Protection Act 2018. This policy applies to all current and former employees, workers, volunteers, interns, apprentices, and consultants.

The company carries out an annual Cyber Security Audit to review and strengthen our data protection controls. Staff are trained on their data protection responsibilities as part of induction.

This policy does not form part of your contract of employment and may be updated from time to time. You should read it alongside your contract and any privacy notice we issue to you.

The Six Principles

All personal data we handle must be:

  • Processed fairly, lawfully, and transparently.
  • Collected for a specific, legitimate purpose and not used for anything else.
  • Adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary.
  • Accurate and kept up to date.
  • Kept only for as long as necessary.
  • Kept secure.

What is personal data?

Personal data is any information that can identify a living individual, either on its own or in combination with other information. It includes opinions about someone and intentions toward them. It does not include anonymised data.

Special category data

Some types of data require extra care. These include information about:

  • Racial or ethnic origin.
  • Political opinions.
  • Religious or philosophical beliefs.
  • Trade union membership.
  • Genetic or biometric data.
  • Health.
  • Sex life or sexual orientation.
  • Criminal convictions or offences.

We will only process special category data where we have a lawful basis to do so, as detailed in your privacy notice.

How we use your data

We process personal data to:

  • Perform your contract of employment.
  • Comply with legal obligations.
  • Pursue our legitimate business interests, where these are not overridden by your rights.

We will not use your data for unrelated purposes without telling you. We do not carry out automated decision making or profiling in relation to employees.

Who we share data with

We may share your data with trusted third-party service providers who assist us in running the business, including our payroll provider, accountant, and outsourced HR provider. All such parties are required to handle your data securely and only for the purpose for which it was shared. We do not transfer personal data outside the UK or European Economic Area without appropriate safeguards in place. If this changes, we will inform you.

Your responsibilities

Everyone who works for us shares responsibility for handling personal data correctly. You must:

  • Only access personal data you need for your work, and only if authorised to do so.
  • Never share personal data informally or with unauthorised people.
  • Keep personal data secure at all times.
  • Use strong passwords and lock your screen when away from your desk.
  • Not save personal data to personal devices.
  • Store paper records securely and shred them when no longer needed.
  • Report any suspected data breach to the Chair & CEO immediately.
  • Not remove or transfer personal data outside of authorised company systems without prior approval.

A deliberate or negligent breach of this policy may result in disciplinary action, including dismissal. Concealing or destroying data that is subject to a Subject Access Request is a criminal offence and gross misconduct.

Data breaches

If you become aware of a data breach, report it to the Chair & CEO immediately and preserve any evidence. If a breach is likely to affect the rights and freedoms of individuals, we are legally required to notify the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) within 72 hours.

Your rights as a data subject

You have the right to:

  • Be informed about how your data is used.
  • Access your personal data via a Subject Access Request (SAR).
  • Have inaccurate data corrected.
  • Request deletion of data we are no longer entitled to hold.
  • Restrict processing while accuracy or lawfulness is contested.
  • Object to processing based on legitimate interests.
  • Object to processing for direct marketing.
  • Receive your data in a portable format.
  • Not be subject to solely automated decisions.
  • Be notified of a security breach affecting your data.

To exercise any of these rights, contact the Chair & CEO in writing. We will respond within one month (or up to three months for complex requests). SARs are free, unless manifestly unfounded or excessive, in which case we may charge a reasonable fee.

You also have the right to complain to the ICO at www.ico.org.uk.

15 Dignity at Work

Everyone has the right to work in an environment of mutual respect. We will not tolerate harassment or bullying of any kind.

What is harassment?

Harassment is any unwanted behaviour that affects a person's dignity or creates a hostile, intimidating, or offensive environment. It can occur because of:

  • Race, nationality, or ethnic origin.
  • Sex or sexual orientation.
  • Religion or political beliefs.
  • Disability or neurodivergence.
  • Age.
  • Gender reassignment.
  • Trade union membership.
  • Any other protected characteristic under the Equality Act 2010.

Forms of harassment include physical contact, verbal abuse, written messages, online behaviour, social exclusion, and bullying.

What to do if you are being harassed

If you feel comfortable doing so, tell the person that their behaviour is unwelcome and must stop. If you do not feel safe doing this, or if the behaviour continues, report it to the Chair & CEO immediately.

Keep a written record of incidents, including dates, times, locations, and what happened. Make notes as soon as possible after each incident.

All complaints will be dealt with promptly, seriously, and in confidence. There will be no victimisation of anyone who raises a genuine concern.

20 Flexible Working

We recognise that flexibility in working arrangements is important to many people. This policy sets out how we handle statutory flexible working requests.

Making a request

All employees may make up to two flexible working requests in any 12-month period. You must wait until any previous request (including any appeal) has been fully resolved before submitting another.

Your request must be in writing and must include:

  • The change you are requesting and when you would like it to take effect.
  • A statement confirming this is a statutory request.
  • Details of any previous flexible working request and when it was made.

Types of change you may request include reduced or compressed hours, working from home, different start and finish times, different working days, or a job share arrangement.

What happens next

Your line manager will meet with you as soon as reasonably possible. The full process, including any appeal, will normally be completed within two months of your request, unless you both agree to extend this.

At the meeting, we will assess how the proposed arrangement could work in practice, discuss the impact on the team and business, and explore any alternatives that might suit both parties.

Approval and contract changes

If your request is approved, the change will be made on a trial or fixed term basis initially. A permanent change to your contract of employment will only be made following a formal review and written agreement from both parties. Any such change will be confirmed in writing.

Each request is considered individually. Approving one person's request does not set a precedent for other employees.

Rejection

A request may be refused for legitimate business reasons, including:

  • Additional cost burden.
  • Inability to reorganise work among existing staff.
  • Inability to recruit suitable cover.
  • Detrimental impact on quality or performance.
  • Inability to meet customer demand.
  • Insufficient work during the proposed hours.
  • A planned structural change to the business.

If your request is refused, we will write to you explaining the business reasons and setting out your right to appeal.

26 Menopause

We want to support all employees through significant life events, including the menopause. This policy should be read alongside our flexible working, sickness absence, and other relevant policies.

We recognise that menopause can affect trans men, non-binary people, and others who do not identify as women. This policy applies equally to all employees who experience menopause, regardless of gender identity. All support, adjustments, and conversations will be handled with the same sensitivity and confidentiality.

The menopause is a natural biological process, typically occurring around the age of 51, though it can happen earlier due to medical treatment or illness. There are three stages:

  • Perimenopause: the years leading up to the menopause, during which symptoms may first appear.
  • Menopause: typically confirmed after 12 consecutive months without a period.
  • Post menopause: the period following this.

Symptoms vary widely but can include hot flushes, night sweats, headaches, fatigue, joint pain, palpitations, anxiety, low mood, difficulty concentrating, and sleep disruption.

Support and adjustments

We encourage anyone experiencing difficulties to speak to their line manager. Reasonable adjustments we can consider include:

  • Time off for GP or specialist appointments, including menopause clinics.
  • Flexibility around start and finish times where fatigue or sleep disruption is a factor.
  • Adjustments to duties or hours where concentration is affected.
  • An additional set of event uniform where attendance at company events is required.
  • Regular check ins for additional support.

All conversations will be treated with sensitivity and in confidence.

Age related hormonal decline in men

Some men experience a gradual decline in testosterone levels with age, typically from their 40s or 50s onwards. This is sometimes referred to as the male menopause, andropause, or late onset hypogonadism. Unlike female menopause, this tends to occur gradually rather than suddenly.

Symptoms may include fatigue, low mood, reduced concentration, changes in sleep, reduced libido, and physical changes. Where these symptoms have a substantial and long-term effect on day-to-day activities, they may constitute a disability under the Equality Act 2010, in which case the company's duty to make reasonable adjustments will apply.

The company encourages any employee experiencing these symptoms to speak to their line manager or the Chair & CEO in confidence. The same support, flexibility, and reasonable adjustments available under this policy apply equally. All conversations will be handled with sensitivity and without judgement.

30 Resolving Problems

We believe clear, fair, and open procedures for resolving problems are essential for everyone. We encourage people to raise concerns early and informally wherever possible.

Grievance Procedure

Stage 1 – Informal

Raise the issue with the relevant person directly and promptly. Many concerns can be resolved quickly through a straightforward conversation.

Stage 2 – Formal

If informal resolution fails or the matter is serious, set out your complaint in writing to your line manager, including as much detail as possible. We will invite you to a meeting to discuss the issue and how it can be resolved. A second meeting may be needed.

We will write to you with the outcome and remind you of your right to appeal. The appeal decision is final. Where the grievance involves your line manager, another manager, director, or independent consultant will handle the investigation.

If your line manager is the subject of your grievance, please submit your complaint directly to the Chair & CEO, who will arrange for it to be handled independently.

If your employment has ended and you wish to raise a grievance, write to your line manager setting out your complaint.

Right to be accompanied

You may bring a work colleague or a certified trade union official to any disciplinary or grievance hearing. Your companion may speak on your behalf and respond to points raised but may not answer questions put directly to you. If they cannot attend on the scheduled date, we will postpone for up to five working days.

Investigations and Suspension

Where necessary, we will carry out an investigation before taking any disciplinary action. We may appoint an independent consultant for impartiality. We may also suspend you on full basic pay during an investigation. Suspension is not a disciplinary sanction and does not imply guilt.

Disciplinary Procedure

This procedure is designed to help you meet the standards we expect and to deal fairly with situations where conduct or performance falls short.

Before any disciplinary action is taken, you will always be:

  • Given a full opportunity to respond to any allegation.
  • Told what action is being considered and what the consequences could be.
  • Advised of any improvement required and the timescale for it.
  • Entitled to be accompanied as described above.

Disciplinary hearings are normally conducted by the Chair & CEO.

Where the Chair & CEO has a direct involvement in the matter, an independent consultant will be appointed to conduct the hearing to ensure a fair and impartial process.

Stage 1 – Informal counselling

For minor concerns, your manager will speak with you informally to help you understand what needs to improve.

Stage 2 – Verbal warning

Where informal counselling has not resolved the issue, or for more serious matters, a verbal warning will be issued. This will be confirmed in writing and remain on your file for up to six months.

Stage 3 – Written warning

For more serious matters, or where a verbal warning has not led to improvement, a written warning will be issued. It will set out the required standards, any timescale for improvement, and the consequences of further misconduct. It will remain on file for up to 12 months.

Stage 4 – Final written warning

Where the problem has not been resolved, or for a serious matter, a final written warning will be issued. This will make clear that dismissal will follow if there is further misconduct or failure to improve. It will remain on file for up to 12 months.

Stage 5 – Dismissal or other serious action

Where all previous stages have been exhausted, or in cases of gross misconduct, you may be dismissed. In some cases, we may instead apply a demotion, disciplinary transfer, or unpaid suspension. Dismissal for gross misconduct will normally be without notice or pay in lieu of notice.

Depending on the circumstances, we may omit or accelerate stages of this procedure.

  • Poor timekeeping.
  • Unreasonable or unauthorised absence.
  • Failure to meet performance standards.
  • Failure to follow company procedures.
  • Minor health and safety breaches.
  • Improper use of company property.

Gross Misconduct (examples)

The following are examples of behaviour that may result in immediate dismissal:

  • Theft, fraud, or bribery.
  • Falsification of records.
  • Breach of confidentiality.
  • Physical assault or fighting.
  • Insubordination or refusal to follow a reasonable instruction.
  • Serious or deliberate damage to company or client property.
  • Serious health and safety breaches.
  • Bringing yourself or the company into disrepute.
  • Bullying, victimisation, or harassment (in person, online, or via any other channel).
  • Attending work under the influence of alcohol or drugs.
  • Possession or use of illegal drugs during working hours or at any work-related event.
  • Verbal abuse or threats of violence.
  • Unauthorised or covert recording of meetings.
  • Serious breaches of our data protection or IT policies.

These are examples only. Behaviour of a similar nature may also be treated as gross misconduct.

Bullying and Harassment

We have zero tolerance for bullying and harassment. Any employee who believes they are being subjected to or has witnessed such behaviour must inform their line manager.

Where possible, ask the person to stop directly or with support from a colleague. If this is not appropriate or does not work, raise a formal complaint with your line manager. The complaint will be treated seriously, investigated confidentially, and dealt with under the disciplinary procedure. There will be no victimisation of anyone who raises a genuine concern in good faith.

Appeals

You have the right to appeal any disciplinary decision. Submit your appeal in writing within five working days of the decision, stating your reasons. You will receive a response within a further five working days. The outcome of the appeal is final.

Performance Management

Where performance falls short of what your role requires, we will work with you openly and constructively to address it. 42 Whistleblowing

The company is committed to the highest standards of openness, honesty, and accountability. This policy is intended to encourage and enable employees to raise genuine concerns about wrongdoing internally, rather than ignoring them or raising them externally in the first instance.

Protected disclosures

Under the Public Interest Disclosure Act 1998, employees have the right to make a protected disclosure — commonly referred to as whistleblowing — without suffering any detriment as a result. A protected disclosure is a disclosure of information that you reasonably believe, and that is made in the public interest, tends to show one or more of the following:

  • A criminal offence has been, is being, or is likely to be committed.
  • A person has failed, is failing, or is likely to fail to comply with a legal obligation.
  • A miscarriage of justice has occurred, is occurring, or is likely to occur.
  • The health or safety of any individual has been, is being, or is likely to be endangered.
  • The environment has been, is being, or is likely to be damaged.
  • Information tending to show any of the above has been, is being, or is likely to be deliberately concealed.

How to raise a concern

If you have a genuine concern about any of the above, please raise it with the Chair & CEO as soon as possible. You should set out your concern in writing where possible, including the facts as you understand them and why you believe a wrongdoing has occurred or is likely to occur.

All disclosures will be taken seriously, handled sensitively, and investigated promptly. Where appropriate, you will be kept informed of the outcome. Your identity will be protected to the extent possible, having regard to the need to investigate effectively.

Protection from detriment

Any employee who makes a protected disclosure in good faith will not suffer any detriment as a result. This includes dismissal, demotion, disciplinary action, harassment, or any other unfavourable treatment. Any employee who subjects another employee to detriment for making a protected disclosure will face disciplinary action, which may include dismissal.

If you believe you have suffered a detriment as a result of making a protected disclosure, please raise this immediately with the Chair & CEO or, if necessary, contact the Advisory, Conciliation and Arbitration Service (ACAS) or an employment tribunal.

Relationship with confidentiality

Nothing in the company's confidentiality or data protection policies prevents you from making a protected disclosure. Your obligation to maintain confidentiality does not apply where you are making a disclosure that is protected under the Public Interest Disclosure Act 1998.

External disclosures

In most cases, concerns should be raised internally in the first instance. However, if you have raised a concern and it has not been addressed, or if you reasonably believe that raising it internally would not be appropriate, you may raise it with a prescribed body such as the Health and Safety Executive, the Information Commissioner's Office, or another relevant regulator.

Purpose and proportionality

This requirement is implemented to support communication, collaboration, professionalism and positive team culture within a remote working environment. It is not intended to constitute surveillance, intrusive monitoring or a means of performance management.

The company will not use video presence as a proxy for productivity or performance, and camera use alone will not form the basis of any formal assessment of an employee's work.

Non compliance

Employees are expected to comply with reasonable management instructions and established working practices. Where an employee is unable to use their camera, they should inform their line manager as soon as reasonably practicable and, where relevant, provide appropriate context so that support can be considered.

Repeated failure to use video without explanation or engagement with the support process available will be addressed in accordance with the company's disciplinary procedure, beginning with an informal conversation in the first instance. This requirement will not be treated as a matter of gross misconduct unless associated with a broader pattern of serious misconduct.
